# DATA.md: data sourcing, construction and test design

## 1. Summary

- **No training data was supplied**, so all data is **synthetic**, generated by `src/data_diverse.py` and written to `data/synthetic_complaints1.csv`.
- About **11,900 rows** (train 7,346 / dev 2,284 / test 2,284), 12 labels, 7 products.
- Splits are made **per scenario, not per row**, so held-out text is a situation the model has never seen, not a re-skin of a training template.
- The classifier is a TF-IDF + logistic regression model. Safety and business rules (C1 to C6) live in a separate rule engine (`src/rules1.py`), not in the data.
- Held-out Macro-F1 on synthetic `test`: **0.878** (train-only model) and **0.886** (final train+dev model, scored once). **These numbers are optimistic for the hidden set**; section 9 explains why and what I expect to break.

**Product validity by label.**

| Label | Valid products |
|---|---|

| `drainage_or_blockage` | washer, dishwasher, refrigerator (defrost drain), AC (condensate drain) |

**Row generation.** Each row combines a scenario paraphrase with a product noun and then adds:

1. **Discourse variety.** Reporting frames ("I'm having an issue: ", "Could someone check why "), observation clauses ("It was working normally before."), and conservative surface rewrites ("does not" to "doesn't", "very loud" to "really noisy").
2. **Wrapper.** Opener, a label-neutral irrelevant detail ("I moved flats in March") and a closer ("pls fix asap"). About 15% of rows are longer narratives with 1 to 2 background details. This is kept light on purpose, so filler does not bury the symptom.

## 4. How the generator evolved, and why

The first generator (`src/data.py`, about 1,700 rows) gave a held-out Macro-F1 of only about 0.53 (train-only) and 0.61 (after adding dev scenarios). Error analysis on its held-out splits found specific causes, and each led to a data change:

| Problem found | Cause | Change |
|---|---|---|
| `not_heating` collapsed on dev (F1 0.03) | AC in heat mode existed as a single scenario; when held out, the model read AC as "cooling" | Added AC heat-mode scenarios and multi-appliance "never generates heat" scenarios |
| `no_power` recall about 0.40 | "no lights on console" was absorbed by `control_panel_or_display` | Added unambiguous whole-unit death scenarios ("draws zero current", "tested the wall outlet, unit is dead"); rewrote control-panel scenarios to say the unit is powered but the screen or buttons fail |
| `water_leak` recall about 0.46 | Confused with `drainage_or_blockage` | More scenarios separating "water on the floor" from "will not drain" |
| `other` recall about 0.49 | Vague or resolved tickets pulled into mechanical labels | Added invoice, warranty, rescheduling enquiries, "issue fixed, thanks" confirmations and generic "unit broke down" tickets |
| Few electrical scenarios outside washers | C1 is the highest-cost rule | Added appliance-specific scenarios: AC inverter board faults, dishwasher short tripping the RCD, oven element short or terminal spark, water heater element tripping the MCB or tingling shock |
| Filler drowned the symptom | Up to 3 paragraphs of filler | Cut filler to 1 to 2 background details |
| Volume | 144 rows per label | Raised the target to about 1,000 rows per label |

**Honest caveat.** These changes were made after looking at dev and test errors. That means the synthetic `test` split is no longer fully independent of our design choices, and the jump from about 0.61 to about 0.88 is partly the generator learning what our own held-out split rewards. It is not a clean estimate of hidden-set performance. See section 9.

- **C1 beats Appendix B wording.** Appendix B lists "trips the supply" under `no_power`, but rule C1 says a tripped supply is `electrical_or_control_board`. Tripped breaker, MCB, RCD or fuse complaints are labelled `electrical_or_control_board`. `no_power` examples avoid tripping language.

- **C6 and `other`.** Thank-you notes (including the hard negative "the leak was fixed, thanks"), sales and price enquiries, spam, how-to questions, status enquiries, gibberish, and complaints with too little evidence ("it stopped working yesterday").
- **C1 vocabulary in the data.** Spark, smoke and burning-smell scenarios sit in `electrical_or_control_board`, and some `overlap` scenarios mix them with another symptom, so the model learns the category. The model is **not** responsible for escalation.

## 6. Split strategy (leakage control)

Splits are assigned **per scenario, never per row**. Every row of a scenario goes to the same split, and the generator asserts that no scenario spans two splits.

1. Roughly 20% of each label's scenarios go to `test`, 20% to `dev`, and the rest to `train` (overall about 62 / 19 / 19 by rows).
2. Each non-plain `kind` (`code`, `overlap`, `ambiguous`, `indirect`) with two or more scenarios in a label is spread across splits, so those behaviours are evaluated, not only learned. At least one scenario of each such kind stays in `train`.
3. **Broad product coverage.** At least one broad scenario (valid for 4 or more products) stays in `train` for each label, so training never lacks a valid product for a fault.
4. `scenario_id` is opaque and used only for split checks, `GroupKFold` and error analysis.

## 7. How the data is used to test

**Protocol** (`python -m src.classifier1`):

1. Tune the regularisation strength `C` on `dev` using a **train-only** model. Dev Macro-F1 is flat across `C` from 1 to 30 (0.827 to 0.829), so the model is not sensitive to this choice.
2. Fit the final model on `train + dev` (about 7 seconds on a laptop CPU, far inside the 5-minute budget).
3. Score it **once** on `test`. `test` is never used for tuning.

**Model.** Word 1 to 2-gram TF-IDF, character 3 to 5-gram TF-IDF (handles typos), and product-crossed features for thermal terms only (so "cold" on a washer differs from "cold" on a fridge), feeding a class-balanced multinomial logistic regression. Error codes are collapsed to one token so unseen codes still generalise.

## 8. How the data interacts with the decision pipeline

1. **Model predicts the category** from text and product.
2. **Rules override or annotate it** (`src/rules1.py`):
   - C1: spark, smoke, burning smell, fire, shock or tripped supply gives `electrical_or_control_board` and `escalate = true`.
   - C2: gas smell on `oven_or_cooktop` or `water_heater` sets `escalate = true`; the category follows the rest of the text.
   - C3: age 30 days or less plus a fit, levelling, connection or first-use complaint gives `installation_or_setup`.
   - C4: a bare error code gives `control_panel_or_display`.
   - C5: if the top two classes are within a margin of 0.08, take the earlier label in Appendix B and lower the confidence.
   - C6: not a complaint gives `other` with confidence below 0.3.
3. Diffuse probabilities (best class below 0.20) map to `other`.

Because rules sit outside the model, the safety behaviour does not depend on how well the synthetic data happens to cover it.

## 9. What I expect to break on real data

I expect the hidden-set score to be **lower than 0.88**, and I want to be clear about why.

1. **Synthetic text is more regular than real text.** Scenarios are hand-written, so real complaints will use phrasing, abbreviations, complaint structures and irrelevant detail I never wrote. Held-out *scenarios* test generalisation to new situations, but all scenarios still come from the same author's imagination.
2. **Design leakage.** The generator was revised after looking at dev and test errors (section 4), so the synthetic test split flatters us. The first-generation score (about 0.53 to 0.61) is arguably closer to what a model faces on text it was not designed around.
3. **Keyword-based safety triggers.** The C1 trigger list is a fixed vocabulary. It can miss misspelled or non-English safety words (for example transliterated words for smoke or spark), and it will fire on negations ("no sparks, no smoke") and loosely related uses ("fire" in "fire station"). I accept the false positives, since a missed safety escalation is far more costly than an unnecessary one.
4. **Class balance.** Our classes are roughly balanced. Real traffic is not: `not_cooling`, `water_leak` and `noise_or_vibration` will dominate, `other` will be a different mix (spam, enquiries, abuse), and the hidden set's Macro-F1 will be sensitive to rare classes.
5. **Error codes.** Codes are all collapsed to one token, so I cannot map specific codes to specific faults. Real manufacturer codes mean different things per brand, and unseen codes are treated as a bare code (C4) unless a symptom is present.
6. **Label disagreement.** Our labels for ambiguous cases follow C5 strictly. A human labeller for the hidden set may pick differently, especially for `water_leak` vs `drainage_or_blockage`, `no_power` vs `control_panel_or_display`, and `not_heating` vs `motor_or_drive`.
7. **Product semantics.** I cross products only with thermal terms. Unusual product-fault pairs, unknown product values, or products outside Appendix A will fall back to text-only evidence.
8. **Long and messy input.** Very long narratives, pasted email threads, and text with signatures or timestamps may dilute the symptom more than our light filler does.
